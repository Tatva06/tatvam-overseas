SS Sheet Weight Calculation: Formula, Density and Chart for SS 304 and SS 202

T
Tatvam Overseas Inc Technical Team Published: 2026-11-19  |  Last updated: 2026-11-19

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Weight (kg) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (mm) × Density (g/cm³)
  • ✓ SS 304 density: 7.93 g/cm³  |  SS 202 density: 7.80 g/cm³
  • ✓ These are theoretical weights. Actual weight varies slightly because of thickness tolerance and edge trimming.

Knowing the weight of a stainless steel sheet helps you estimate cost, plan transport and check what you receive. The calculation is simple once you know the formula and the density of the grade.

The Formula

Use this formula for the theoretical weight of a flat sheet:

Weight (kg) = Length (m) × Width (m) × Thickness (mm) × Density (g/cm3)

The units work out neatly: a sheet of 1 m × 1 m × 1 mm contains 1,000 cm3 of steel, so its weight in kg equals the density number.

Density Values

  • SS 304: about 7.93 g/cm3
  • SS 202: about 7.80 g/cm3

These are commonly used values. Small differences between mills and compositions exist, so use the figures on your Mill Test Certificate for exact work.

Worked Example

A 4 ft × 8 ft sheet of SS 304, 1.0 mm thick:

  • 4 ft = 1.2192 m and 8 ft = 2.4384 m, so the area is about 2.973 m2
  • Weight = 2.973 × 1.0 × 7.93 = about 23.58 kg

These are theoretical weights. Actual sheet weight varies slightly because of thickness tolerance and edge trimming, so treat the figures as estimates for planning, not for final billing.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing units. Convert feet and inches to metres before using the formula.
  • Using one density for every grade. Different grades have slightly different densities.
  • Forgetting tolerance. Real sheets can be a little under or over nominal thickness.
  • Using sheet weight for pipes or bars. Those need different formulas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does SS 202 weigh slightly less than SS 304? ↓
Its density is a little lower because of its different composition.
Can I use this for other sheet sizes? ↓
Yes. Multiply the area of any sheet in square metres by the thickness in mm and the density.
