SS 202 vs SS 304: Difference, Price & Which to Buy

T
Tatvam Overseas Inc Technical Team Published: 2026-10-21  |  Last updated: 2026-11-19
Short Answer: Which Grade is Better?

SS 304 is the superior metallurgical grade with 8.0–10.5% nickel and 17.5–19.5% chromium, making it the industry standard for food handling, commercial kitchens, outdoor cladding, and moist environments. SS 202 is an economical alternative where nickel is lowered (4.0–6.0%) and replaced by manganese; choose SS 202 strictly for dry indoor decorative panels, furniture, and indoor architectural trims where direct exposure to moisture, chlorides, or food acids is absent.

Key Technical Takeaways

  • ✓ SS 202 belongs to the 200-series austenitic stainless steels, where manganese and nitrogen replace part of the nickel.
  • ✓ SS 304 contains about 18% chromium and 8 to 10.5% nickel, forming a resilient, self-healing passive film.
  • ✓ SS 202 performs reliably in dry indoor environments, indoor balustrades, hotel furniture, and utensils when properly cleaned and maintained.
  • ✓ In outdoor, coastal, chemical, or continuous wet applications, SS 202 can develop tea-staining and pitting; SS 304 or SS 316L is necessary.
  • ✓ Visual appearance and magnet tests cannot differentiate annealed 202 from 304; always inspect the EN 10204 3.1 MTC heat number and verify composition via PMI on request.

In Indian steel procurement and architectural fabrication, the dilemma between SS 202 sheets and SS 304 sheets is one of the most common buying questions. Both grades belong to the austenitic stainless family and share a similar initial appearance, yet their metallurgical composition, corrosion resistance, and total lifecycle costs differ substantially.

What is SS 202 stainless steel?

SS 202 is an austenitic grade belonging to the 200-series (chromium-nickel-manganese) family, standardized under ASTM A240 UNS S20200 and Indian Standard IS 6911. It was engineered to reduce reliance on costly nickel by employing manganese and nitrogen as austenite stabilizers. This metallurgy gives 202 good formability, tensile strength, and aesthetic appeal at a significantly lower initial raw material cost than 300-series alloys.

SS 202 composition and nickel percentage

Under the ASTM A240 specification, standard SS 202 contains 17.0% to 19.0% Chromium and 4.0% to 6.0% Nickel, with 7.5% to 10.0% Manganese and up to 0.25% Nitrogen. Commercial Indian market variants, such as Jindal J1 and J4, are custom sub-chemistries where nickel is balanced with added copper to optimize press forming for utensils and interior panels. By contrast, ASTM A240 SS 304 contains 8.0% to 10.5% Nickel and 17.5% to 19.5% Chromium. The higher nickel content in 304 provides higher ductility and a more stable, self-repairing chromium oxide passive barrier. Is SS 202 rust proof?

No stainless steel is completely immune to corrosion. SS 202 forms a protective chromium passive film under dry, clean atmospheric conditions. However, because manganese compounds replace nickel, the passive barrier is significantly less stable when exposed to chlorides, salt spray, atmospheric sulfur dioxide, or standing water.

In outdoor, coastal (such as Mumbai, Chennai, or Kochi), or humid environments, SS 202 develops superficial brown staining ("tea-staining") that can progress into localized pitting corrosion. For exterior architectural cladding or coastal installations, SS 304 or molybdenum-bearing SS 316L is essential.

SS 202 vs SS 304 for kitchen use

In kitchen and catering environments, the distinction between wet food contact and dry structural components is critical:

  • Wet prep tables, sinks, dishwashing units, and cookware: SS 304 is strictly recommended. Food salts, vinegar, citric acids, and chemical dishwashing detergents can break down the passive film of SS 202, causing pitting where bacteria can lodge. SS 304 meets standard international hygiene and food-contact criteria.
  • Dry storage racks, cabinet doors, and display panels: SS 202 can be effectively utilized for dry storage shelves, dining furniture frames, and decorative partition screens, where routine contact with liquid food and acidic condiments is absent.

How to identify SS 202 vs SS 304

Material mix-ups can be costly. When verifying shipments of stainless steel sheets, pipes, or plates, follow these testing guidelines:

1. The Magnet Test Fallacy

A magnet test cannot reliably differentiate SS 202 from SS 304. Both alloys are austenitic and non-magnetic in their fully annealed state. When sheared, bent, or deep-drawn, both grades undergo strain-induced transformation to martensite and show mild magnetic pull. Never reject or accept material based on a magnet alone.

2. Positive Material Identification (PMI) & XRF Testing

Handheld X-ray Fluorescence (XRF) spectroscopy provides nondestructive elemental analysis in seconds. A certified PMI gun instantly measures nickel (8–10.5% for 304 vs 4–6% for 202) and manganese percentages, verifying the heat chemistry on site.

3. Mill Test Certificates (EN 10204 3.1)

Every prime order must be accompanied by an authentic manufacturer inspection certificate showing chemical melt analysis, mechanical tensile values, and lot heat numbers matching the stencil on the sheet. Learn how to verify certificates in our guide on EN 10204 3.1 vs 3.2 mill test certificates.

Frequently Asked Questions: SS 202 vs SS 304

What is SS 202 stainless steel? ↓
SS 202 is an austenitic chromium-nickel-manganese stainless steel conforming to ASTM A240 (UNS S20200). It replaces part of the expensive nickel content with manganese (7.5-10.0%) and nitrogen (up to 0.25%), lowering production cost while maintaining an austenitic structure.
What is the nickel percentage in SS 202 vs SS 304? ↓
SS 202 contains 4.0% to 6.0% nickel (with some commercial variants lower), whereas standard SS 304 contains 8.0% to 10.5% nickel. Nickel stabilizes the austenitic crystal structure and enhances corrosion resistance and ductility.
Is SS 202 rust proof? ↓
No stainless steel is completely rust-proof. SS 202 forms a protective chromium oxide layer in dry indoor conditions, but its lower nickel and higher manganese make it susceptible to tea-staining and pitting in humid, coastal, or chloride-rich environments.
Which is better for kitchen use: SS 202 or SS 304? ↓
For commercial kitchens, sinks, food contact surfaces, and wet counters, SS 304 is the recommended choice due to superior resistance to organic acids, food salts, and detergents. SS 202 is widely used for domestic dry utensils, cabinet framing, and dining furniture where moisture contact is minimal.
Can a magnet distinguish SS 202 from SS 304? ↓
No. A handheld magnet cannot reliably tell 202 from 304. Both are austenitic and non-magnetic in the annealed condition, and both can exhibit mild surface magnetism after cold forming, shearing, or bending. Positive Material Identification (PMI) via handheld XRF or mill test certificates (EN 10204 3.1) must be used.
What is the price difference between SS 202 and SS 304 sheets? ↓
SS 202 is significantly more economical than SS 304 because it contains roughly half the nickel. However, using SS 202 in aggressive or outdoor environments can lead to premature maintenance or replacement costs.
