SS 202 vs SS 304: Differences, Uses and Which Grade to Buy

T
Tatvam Overseas Inc Technical Team Published: 2026-10-21  |  Last updated: 2026-10-21
Direct Answer

Quick answer: SS 202 is an austenitic grade that substitutes manganese and nitrogen for a significant portion of nickel, lowering material cost while preserving formability. SS 304 contains 8.0% to 10.5% nickel and 18.0% to 20.0% chromium, providing a stronger passive oxide layer that resists rust in wet, outdoor, and food-processing environments. Choose SS 202 for dry indoor decorative elements; choose SS 304 for food contact, exterior architecture, and demanding industrial service.

Key Technical Takeaways

  • ✓ SS 202 belongs to the 200 series austenitic stainless steels, using manganese (7.5–10%) and nitrogen to stabilize the austenite crystal lattice with lower nickel (1.0–4.0%).
  • ✓ SS 304 (18/8) provides 18–20% Chromium and 8–10.5% Nickel, creating a resilient, self-healing chromium oxide film that resists pitting and atmospheric corrosion.
  • ✓ SS 202 performs reliably in dry indoor environments, indoor balustrades, hotel furniture, and utensils when properly cleaned and maintained.
  • ✓ In outdoor, coastal, chemical, or continuous wet applications, SS 202 will suffer rapid tea-staining and pitting; SS 304 or SS 316L is necessary.
  • ✓ Visual appearance and magnet tests cannot differentiate annealed 202 from 304; always inspect the EN 10204 3.1 MTC heat number and verify composition via PMI on request.

In architectural metal fabrication and commercial kitchen manufacturing, few questions arise as frequently as: Should I specify SS 202 or SS 304? On a warehouse floor or display rack, both grades look identical with matching reflectivity and feel. However, the metallurgical trade-offs between their alloy additions directly dictate longevity, maintenance requirements, and corrosion resistance.

1. Chemical Composition: The Nickel vs. Manganese Balance

Both Grade 202 and Grade 304 are austenitic stainless steels featuring a face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure. The critical distinction lies in how that austenitic structure is stabilized:

  • Grade 304 (UNS S30400 / 1.4301): Stabilizes austenite primarily using 8.00% to 10.50% Nickel, paired with 18.00% to 20.00% Chromium.
  • Grade 202 (UNS S20200 / IS 6911): Conserves expensive nickel by substituting it with 7.50% to 10.00% Manganese and up to 0.25% Nitrogen, keeping nickel levels between 1.00% and 4.00% depending on sub-grade specifications (such as J4 or standard ASTM A240).

2. Corrosion Resistance: Why 304 Outperforms 202 in Hostile Media

The corrosion resistance of any stainless steel relies on the integrity of its microscopic passive film. While SS 202 contains sufficient chromium (17–19%) to passivate under clean, dry conditions, the presence of manganese and lower nickel makes that passive layer more susceptible to chemical breakdown.

In environments with humidity, airborne chlorides, industrial sulphur dioxide, or cleaning chemicals, SS 202 can develop localized surface staining ("tea-staining") that progresses into pitting. In contrast, SS 304 maintains a denser, more cohesive passivation layer that self-repairs effectively in atmospheric moisture. For severe marine environments within 5 km of coastlines, even 304 may prove insufficient, requiring molybdenum-bearing SS 316L Marine Grade Plates (as detailed in our SS 304 vs SS 316L Technical Guide).

3. Application Suitability: Where SS 202 Works vs. Where It Fails

Good Applications for SS 202:

  • Indoor stair handrails and decorative balustrades in climate-controlled spaces
  • Commercial hotel and mall interior furniture and display framing
  • Indoor architectural elevator cladding and wall paneling
  • Domestic cookware and dry food storage containers
  • General indoor sheet metal fabrication where moisture exposure is absent

Specify SS 304 Instead When:

  • Exterior building facades, exterior handrails, or outdoor canopies
  • Dairy processing, commercial brewing, and liquid food piping systems
  • Commercial kitchen wet areas, sinks, and dishwashing lines
  • Coastal, marine, or high-humidity geographic installations
  • Pharmaceutical process equipment requiring chemical sanitization

4. Surface Finishes and Fabrication Compatibility

Both grades accommodate standard sheet surface treatments. SS 202 is widely processed with decorative finishes including No.4 brushed satin, hairline, and Physical Vapor Deposition (PVD) color coatings for interior design installations. However, because manganese increases the work-hardening rate, SS 202 requires higher press tonnage during deep drawing and exhibits higher tool wear during machining than SS 304.

5. How to Verify You Received the Grade You Ordered

A common misconception in the marketplace is that a simple handheld magnet can distinguish SS 304 from SS 202. A magnet test is completely unreliable. Both grades are austenitic and non-magnetic in their fully annealed state. Furthermore, both grades will develop slight magnetic attraction when cold worked, sheared, or bent due to mechanical strain-induced martensite formation.

To ensure quality assurance and avoid material mix-ups:

  1. Inspect the EN 10204 3.1 Mill Test Certificate (MTC): Check that the heat number stamped or stenciled on the metal plate matches the certified chemical test values on the certificate exactly. Learn how to audit test documentation in our guide: What is an EN 10204 3.1 Mill Test Certificate?
  2. Request Positive Material Identification (PMI): Handheld X-ray Fluorescence (XRF) or Optical Emission Spectrometry (OES) measures exact percentages of Nickel (8–10% for 304 vs ~1–4% for 202) and Manganese nondestructively in seconds.
  3. Chemical Spot Testing: Specialized chemical reagent test kits can detect manganese levels or nickel depletion via rapid electrochemical color reaction.

6. Summary: Making the Right Commercial Decision

SS 202 is a viable, engineered stainless steel grade when applied in dry, controlled indoor environments where aesthetic appeal and budget discipline are primary criteria. However, using SS 202 in outdoor, wet food, or coastal installations to save upfront procurement costs often leads to premature tea-staining, maintenance downtime, and replacement expenses that far exceed initial savings.

Frequently Asked Buyer & Engineering Questions

Is SS 202 real stainless steel? ↓
Yes. It is an austenitic stainless steel with about 17 to 19% chromium. It has lower corrosion resistance than 304 because part of the nickel is replaced by manganese and nitrogen.
Is SS 202 suitable for food contact? ↓
It is widely used in utensils and kitchenware, but suitability depends on the application and the rules that apply to your product. For dairy, brewing and acidic foods, 304 or 316L is the usual choice. Check your product's requirements.
Will SS 202 rust? ↓
It can show surface rust or staining in humid, salty or acidic conditions, and it is more likely to than 304. Good surface finishing, cleaning and dry indoor use reduce the risk.
How do I check that I received the grade I ordered? ↓
Compare the heat number on the material with the mill test certificate, and ask for PMI on the sheet. A magnet test is not enough.
