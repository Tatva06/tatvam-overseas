EN 10204 3.1 vs 3.2 Certificate: Meaning & Difference

T
Tatvam Overseas Inc Technical Team Published: 2026-10-21  |  Last updated: 2026-11-20
Short Answer: What is an EN 10204 3.1 MTC?

An EN 10204 Type 3.1 Mill Test Certificate (MTC) is a formal inspection document issued by the manufacturer's authorized inspection representative who is independent of the manufacturing department. It confirms that the specific batch of delivered steel products complies with order specifications, based on specific chemical and mechanical testing of the supplied heat.

Key Technical Takeaways

  • ✓ European Standard EN 10204 defines the types of inspection documents required for all metallic products.
  • ✓ An EN 10204 Type 3.1 certificate is formally validated by an authorized quality inspector independent of the production manufacturing hierarchy.
  • ✓ It must record specific test results from the actual heat/melt supplied, not representative or historical averages.
  • ✓ Core data sections include chemical ladle analysis, mechanical tensile and yield tests, impact energy, hardness, and heat treatment logs.
  • ✓ EN 10204 3.2 differs by requiring dual endorsement from both the manufacturer's inspector and an independent third-party inspector (TUV, DNV, BV).

When procuring stainless steel sheets, pipes, flanges, or nickel alloy components, an authentic test certificate is the primary proof of metallurgical integrity. European Standard EN 10204 governs the inspection documents required for metallic products across global engineering projects. Understanding the difference between Type 3.1 and Type 3.2 certificates ensures compliance while avoiding unnecessary inspection costs.

The Four EN 10204 Inspection Document Types

Document Type Definition Who Validates Typical Application
EN 10204 Type 2.1 Declaration of compliance with the order The manufacturer Non-critical commercial steel, general hardware
EN 10204 Type 2.2 Test report based on non-specific inspection The manufacturer Standard stock items, non-critical fabrication
EN 10204 Type 3.1 Inspection certificate based on specific testing of delivered product Manufacturer's independent inspection representative Standard industrial benchmark: pressure vessels, piping, SS sheets, export orders
EN 10204 Type 3.2 Inspection certificate based on specific testing with independent third-party witness Dual validation: Manufacturer representative AND Purchaser inspector or Third-Party Agency (TPI) Critical offshore, nuclear, subsea, defense, or high-risk statutory infrastructure

What does a 3.1 MTC contain?

An EN 10204 3.1 certificate represents specific testing conducted on the exact heat or lot delivered to the customer. A comprehensive 3.1 certificate includes:

  • Manufacturer & Order Identification: Mill name, purchase order number, customer name, and invoice reference.
  • Heat / Melt Number: A unique metallurgical traceability code stamped on the metal plates or pipes corresponding to the batch molten in the furnace.
  • Material Specification & Grade: ASTM, ASME, EN, or IS standard (e.g. ASTM A240 Grade 304/304L or UNS S20200).
  • Chemical Composition Analysis: Exact percentage by weight of alloying elements (Carbon, Manganese, Silicon, Phosphorus, Sulfur, Chromium, Nickel, Molybdenum, Nitrogen).
  • Mechanical Test Results: Yield strength ($R_{p0.2}$), ultimate tensile strength ($R_m$), elongation percentage ($A$), and hardness values (HBW/HRB).
  • Supplementary Quality Tests: Intergranular corrosion test (IGC per ASTM A262), flattening, flaring, hydrostatic, or eddy current tests where mandated by the product standard.
  • Authorized Inspector Sign-off: The stamp and signature of the manufacturer's inspection officer, who operates independently from the manufacturing department.

EN 10204 3.1 vs 3.2: which do you need?

For most industrial projects, Type 3.1 is the standard requirement. It guarantees verified batch-specific testing without the additional lead time and inspection fees associated with external agencies.

You require Type 3.2 only when project specifications, international design codes (such as ASME Section III for nuclear, or offshore subsea pipeline rules), or regulatory authorities explicitly mandate third-party witness inspection. Under Type 3.2, an independent third-party inspection agency (such as SGS, TÜV SÜD, Bureau Veritas, or DNV) attends the mill, witnesses mechanical tensile and impact testing, checks dimensional tolerances, and co-signs the certificate alongside the mill representative.

How to check a mill test certificate

Before accepting stainless steel or alloy stock at your warehouse or job site, perform a systematic 3-step check:

  1. Verify Stencil vs. Certificate: Confirm that the heat number embossed, stenciled, or hard-stamped on the sheet or pipe matches the certificate characters exactly.
  2. Check Composition Limits: Compare the reported chemical percentages against ASTM A240 or relevant standard limits (e.g., verifying 8.0–10.5% Ni for SS 304).
  3. Correlate with Positive Material Identification: For high-value orders, cross-verify the piece using handheld XRF spectroscopy to ensure that pieces were not mixed during cutting or shipping.

Frequently Asked Questions: EN 10204 3.1 & 3.2

What is an EN 10204 3.1 inspection certificate? ↓
An EN 10204 3.1 certificate is a document issued by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative who is independent of the manufacturing department. It certifies that the delivered steel products comply with order specifications based on specific testing.
What is the difference between EN 10204 3.1 and 3.2? ↓
A 3.1 certificate is validated solely by the manufacturer's independent inspection representative. A 3.2 certificate requires dual validation: both the manufacturer's representative and an independent third-party inspector (e.g. SGS, TUV, Bureau Veritas, DNV) or purchaser's designated inspector validate the test results.
Can a stockist or trader issue an EN 10204 3.1 certificate? ↓
No. Under European Standard EN 10204, a 3.1 certificate can only be issued by the original material manufacturing mill. Stockists can only pass on copies of the mill's certified MTC correlated to heat numbers stamped on the metal.
What information must be present on a 3.1 MTC? ↓
A genuine 3.1 MTC contains the manufacturing mill's details, heat/melt number, material standard and grade, chemical analysis (Cr, Ni, Mo, C, etc.), mechanical test results (tensile, yield, elongation), dimensions, and the signature/stamp of the independent inspector.
When is an EN 10204 3.2 certificate mandatory? ↓
A 3.2 certificate is required for critical applications such as nuclear installations, high-pressure subsea pipelines, offshore platforms, and certain PED/boiler applications where statutory codes or end-client specifications mandate third-party witness inspection.
