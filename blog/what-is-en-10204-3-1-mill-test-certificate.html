What is an EN 10204 3.1 Mill Test Certificate? A Practical Engineer's Guide

T
Tatvam Overseas Inc Technical Team Published: 2025-11-20  |  Updated: 2026-10-14

Key Technical Takeaways

  • ✓ European Standard EN 10204 defines the types of inspection documents required for all metallic products.
  • ✓ An EN 10204 Type 3.1 certificate is legally certified by an authorized quality inspector independent of the production manufacturing hierarchy.
  • ✓ It must record specific test results from the actual heat/melt supplied, not representative or historical averages.
  • ✓ Core data sections include chemical ladle analysis, mechanical tensile and yield tests, impact energy, hardness, and heat heat-treatment logs.
  • ✓ EN 10204 3.2 differs by requiring dual endorsement from both the manufacturer's inspector and an independent third-party inspector (TUV, DNV, BV).

In the global steel, piping, and pressure vessel industries, physical steel is only as reliable as the documentation that accompanies it. A Mill Test Certificate (MTC) — also termed a Mill Test Report (MTR) or Inspection Certificate — serves as the legal pedigree of metallic material. Among all inspection document classifications, EN 10204 Type 3.1 is the gold standard demanded by engineering consultants, EPC contractors, and refinery operators worldwide.

1. The Origin of European Standard EN 10204

Published by the European Committee for Standardization (CEN), EN 10204 replaced older DIN 50049 standards to harmonize inspection documentation for metallic goods. The standard classifies certificates into two distinct categories:

  • Non-Specific Inspection (Type 2.1 & Type 2.2): Documents wherein the manufacturer declares products conform to specifications based on non-specific historical quality control, without testing the actual customer lot.
  • Specific Inspection (Type 3.1 & Type 3.2): Documents wherein tests are performed on the actual products supplied, or test units taken directly from the customer's specific production heat batch.

2. Key Requirement: Independent Quality Verification in Type 3.1

The cornerstone of EN 10204 Type 3.1 is statutory organizational independence. The document must be validated and signed by an authorized inspection representative who is completely independent of the manufacturing and production department.

This structural separation guarantees that quality auditors cannot be pressured by production targets or dispatch deadlines to approve borderline or sub-specification steel. The inspector represents the voice of metallurgical compliance, verifying that ladle analysis, tensile yields, and non-destructive evaluations strictly satisfy the governing ASTM, ASME, or EN standards.

3. Anatomy of a Legitimate EN 10204 3.1 Certificate

When auditing an incoming MTC from Tatvam Overseas Inc or any tier-one mill, quality control inspectors examine seven indispensable data clusters:

1 Material Identification & Heat Traceability

Manufacturer name, customer PO number, product dimensions, quantity, weight, and the unique Heat Number. This heat number must match the physical continuous stencil or hard stamping on the pipe, sheet, or flange.

2 Chemical Ladle Analysis (OES / Spectrometry)

Precise percentage by mass of each constituent element (C, Mn, Si, P, S, Cr, Ni, Mo, Ti, Cu, N). Both the standard specification limits and actual heat sample values must be side-by-side.

3 Mechanical & Physical Property Tests

Yield Strength (0.2% offset proof stress in MPa), Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS in MPa), Percentage Elongation (% in 2 inches or 50mm gauge length), and Hardness (HBW, HRC, or HRB).

4 Supplementary & Non-Destructive Tests

Hydrostatic test pressures, eddy current or ultrasonic examination, intergranular corrosion (IGC Practice E), flattening, flaring, and impact test values (Joules at specified temperatures such as -20°C or -46°C).
