SS 202 vs SS 304 Circles: Which to Choose?

Selecting between SS 202 and SS 304 circles depends primarily on operating atmosphere, corrosion requirements, and production budget:

  • SS 304 Circles: SS 304 resists corrosion significantly better and suits food contact and wet use. With its classic 18% chromium and 8% nickel metallurgical matrix, SS 304 provides robust protection against rust and pitting in regular washing, high moisture, and acidic food preparation environments. It is the premier standard for commercial cookware, pressure cooker bodies, dairy equipment, and export-grade kitchen products.
  • SS 202 Circles: SS 202 is more economical and suits dry indoor use. By replacing a major portion of nickel with manganese and nitrogen, SS 202 achieves high mechanical strength and good workability at a significantly lower raw material cost. It is widely utilized for everyday domestic utensils, storage containers, dry service trays, and non-corrosive indoor metalware.

Stainless Steel Circle Weight Calculation & Chart

The theoretical weight of a stainless steel circle is calculated based on its surface area, sheet thickness, and metallurgical density:

Circle Weight (kg) = 0.7854 × [Diameter in metres]² × [Thickness in mm] × Density

Density values: SS 304 has a density of 7.93 g/cm³ (7930 kg/m³), while SS 202 has a density of 7.80 g/cm³ (7800 kg/m³).

Factors That Influence SS Circle Pricing

Stainless steel circle pricing fluctuates based on domestic raw material movements and specific production parameters:

  • Alloy Grade Selection: SS 304 carries a premium over SS 202 because of its 8% nickel content and higher chromium formulation.
  • Diameter & Gauge Thickness: Non-standard diameters or specialized cold-rolled gauges require dedicated tooling setup or circle shearing.
  • Raw Feedstock Indices: Price revisions from major domestic mills (such as Jindal Stainless) influence base coil and sheet costs.
  • Batch Volume: Bulk container and commercial tonnage orders benefit from optimized coil utilization and lower shearing scrap factors.
