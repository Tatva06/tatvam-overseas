How to Read an EN 10204 3.1 Mill Test Certificate (MTC)

An EN 10204 3.1 inspection certificate is the standard quality document validating that steel, stainless steel, or nickel alloy products meet their specified chemical and mechanical requirements. Below is a structured breakdown of each section found on a mill test certificate.

1

Material Identification & Traceability

This header block identifies the primary manufacturer, the buyer purchase order, and establishes the physical traceability of the steel product.

Heat Number (Cast / Melt No.):

The unique batch code assigned during steel melting. It must match the physical stencil, roll mark, or vibro-etching on the pipe, plate, or bar.

Standard & Material Grade:

The governing specification, such as ASTM A240 (SS 304/316L plates), ASTM A312 (seamless pipes), or ASME SA335 (P11/P22 alloy pipes).

Product Dimensions:

Specified thickness, width, length, pipe diameter (OD), wall thickness (WT / Schedule), and weight/piece count.

Certificate Standard:

Explicit statement declaring validation under European Standard EN 10204 Type 3.1 inspection certification.

2

Chemical Composition (Ladle / Spectral Analysis)

The chemical analysis table presents the percentage concentration of each alloying element. Under EN 10204 3.1, the mill provides both the standard permissible boundaries and the actual spectrographic values recorded for that heat.

Element Symbol Role in Stainless & Alloy Steels Inspection Check
Carbon C Controls hardness & weldability; low carbon (≤0.030% in 304L/316L) prevents sensitization. Verify within grade limits
Chromium Cr Forms the passive chromium-oxide protective film (typically 16–20%). Verify within grade limits
Nickel Ni Stabilizes austenitic microstructure, adds ductility and toughness. Verify within grade limits
Molybdenum Mo Enhances resistance to pitting and crevice corrosion in chloride environments (2.0–3.0% in 316L). Verify presence for 316L
Sulfur & Phosphorus S, P Residual impurities kept strictly below maximum thresholds (usually ≤0.030% or ≤0.045%). Confirm below max limit
3

Mechanical & Physical Property Testing

Testing conducted on representative test coupons taken from the production lot to prove structural load capacity and ductility.

Tensile Strength (Rm):

Maximum engineering stress sustained before necking or rupture, reported in MPa (N/mm²) or ksi.

Yield Strength (Rp0.2):

The stress at 0.2% permanent plastic deformation. Crucial design parameter for pressure containment.

Elongation percentage (A%):

Measures post-fracture gauge extension, ensuring the steel has sufficient ductility for bending or forming.

Hardness & Impact (Charpy-V):

Hardness values (HRB/HRC or HBW) and sub-zero impact energy tests when mandated by project engineering specs.

4

Supplementary Tests & Independent Endorsement

Confirms specialized testing, non-destructive examinations (NDT), heat treatment schedules, and official inspector validation.

Heat Treatment Record: Solution annealing temperatures, holding durations, and quench methods (water/air) to restore corrosion resistance.
Non-Destructive Testing (NDT): Hydrostatic test pressure reports, Eddy Current test results, or Ultrasonic (UT) flaw detection reports.
Corrosion Testing (IGC): Intergranular corrosion test results (e.g. ASTM A262 Practice E) when requested for aggressive chemical duty.
PMI & Third-Party Testing: PMI and third-party laboratory testing can be arranged on request.
Inspection Sign-off: Must bear the signature and stamp of the mill's authorized inspection representative independent of the manufacturing shop.

What to Check When Material Arrives

When receiving steel from a stockist or supplier, always cross-reference the heat number stencilled on the material directly with the heat number printed on the EN 10204 3.1 MTC. For cut pieces, request that the heat number is stamped or marked onto each individual section prior to dispatch.
